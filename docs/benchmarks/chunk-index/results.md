# Chunk-index benchmark results (Prompt 08)

The evidence for [ADR 0007](../../decisions/0007-chunk-index-encoding.md).

- **Budgets.** They were fixed before any measurement: [`budgets.md`](budgets.md), in commit `ab5a0fa0`.
- **Method and raw format.** See [`README.md`](README.md).
- **How this file was made.** The tables in §3 are generated by `Summarize` from the six raw files in `raw/`, combined in run order, so a later run's value replaces an earlier one. §1, §2, and §4 are written by hand.

## 1. Conditions

- **Machine.** AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D (16 threads), 30 GiB RAM, an NVMe SSD with ext4 on LVM, Linux 7.2.6, OpenJDK 25.0.4.1.
- **`fsync` floor.** A median 4 KiB write plus `fsync` takes **16 ms** on this filesystem, measured at the start of every run. That is slow for NVMe, so every durable-commit figure here is pessimistic. The floor for any candidate's commit is one `fsync`.
- **Page cache.** The OS page cache is warm. "Cold open" means a new JVM, not a cold disk.
- **Heap.** The `millions` dataset runs with `-Xmx256m`; everything else runs with `-Xmx512m`.

**Reading the tables.**

- **growth/update** is the disk growth per applied update mutation over the 25 000-mutation phase.
  - C1-log's figure includes its checkpoint at the end of that phase. A negative value means the checkpoint compacted log records left over from the build into blocks.
  - C1-log on `friend-server` and `negative-coords` shows about 50 bytes per record after the build. Those datasets stay under the 100 000-mutation checkpoint threshold, so their records were still in the uncompressed log. They drop to about 15 bytes per record after the checkpoint.
- **index heap** is the live heap held by the open index after every workload, measured after full collections. SQLite's page cache is native memory, so it shows in **RSS peak**, not in index heap.

## 4. Observations

1. **Corruption (gate B2) separates the candidates.**
   - SQLite returned silently wrong records for 86 of 300 single-byte flips. 46 of those survived `PRAGMA integrity_check`, and 12 more flips crashed with uncoded exceptions.
   - MVStore was silent on 61 of 300, and 60 survived a full read and cross-check of every map.
   - Every region-block candidate detected every flip that mattered. The rest landed in bytes no reader uses (superseded blocks or slack) and left the export byte-identical.
2. **One file per region is `fsync`-bound.** C1-files took 2.7–6.6 s per 1 000-mutation commit, because it syncs every touched region's file.
3. **Copy-on-write blocks amplify scattered updates.**
   - C1-segments and C3 rewrite every touched region's block and the whole catalog on every commit. That cost 0.2–0.9 s per commit at 0.1–1 M records, and 5 s at 9.5 M.
   - Their first version also cached decoded record objects and **ran out of heap** on `millions` under `-Xmx256m`. The compact cache of inflated bytes plus offsets fixed both problems, and dense uniform lookups fell from 46 µs to 0.5 µs.
4. **Commits must not read.**
   - The first C1-log logged resolved records, so each mutation read its chunk's record first: about 230 ms per commit on `millions`.
   - A GC-logged diagnostic at 256 MiB and 1 GiB heaps gave identical latencies, with a longest pause of 38 ms, so this was I/O and CPU, not memory pressure.
   - Logging the mutations themselves, validated only by existence, brought commits to **15.7 ms p50 and 23.7 ms p99 on 9.5 M records**: one `fdatasync`, whatever the index size.
5. **A log must tell a torn tail from corruption.**
   - The first mutation-log replay stopped at the first bad frame, treating it as a torn tail. It silently dropped every later, acknowledged commit, in 300 of 300 flips on the read path. `verify()` caught all of them.
   - Frames now carry a header checksum over their length. Only an *incomplete* final frame is a torn tail; everything else fails closed. This is the same rule as ADR 0006 for journals. The re-run found 0 silent results.
6. **Uniform random lookups are the region-block weakness at scale.**
   - On `millions`, about 63 µs p50 and 0.72 ms p99, because a cache miss inflates and walks a whole region block. Blocks whose records carry up to 12 seasons of activity inflate to an estimated 100 KB or more; this is computed from the encoding, not measured.
   - SQLite and MVStore answered in 7–10 µs. Local lookups, which are Paper's pattern, take 0.3–0.4 µs on every C1 variant.
7. **Checkpoints of scattered updates rewrite most blocks.**
   - Folding 25 000 uniformly scattered mutations took 0.8–1.5 s at about 1 M records.
   - On `millions` it took 18.5 s, touching nearly every one of 16 000 regions and writing about 150 MB.
   - Localized updates (players' surroundings) touch far fewer regions, but Prompt 09 must make checkpoint and compaction scheduling explicit and measured.
8. **Size.** Region blocks need 3–21 bytes per record, about 3 for a dense legacy square. SQLite needs 48–117. MVStore needs 22–59, and 275 on `millions`, where its file reached 2.6 GB because it isn't compacted during bulk writes.

## 5. Threats to validity

- **One machine and one filesystem.** Its `fsync` cost dominates every commit figure, and Windows and macOS were not measured.
- **Process kill is not power loss.** A `SIGKILL` leaves the page cache intact, so B3 tests atomicity. `fsync` ordering is reviewed by design and must be fault-injected in Prompt 09.
- **Synthetic data.** Real worlds may have different shapes. The datasets deliberately include the dense, sparse, multi-season, revisited, and extreme-coordinate cases.
- **Worst-case access patterns.** The update batches and uniform lookups are spread across the whole world, which is the worst case for region blocks and closer to neutral for B-trees. Local lookups are reported separately; no local update workload was measured.
- **Spike implementations are not tuned equally.**
  - SQLite runs with a 64 MiB page cache and cached point-lookup statements.
  - MVStore runs with LZF compression and a 64 MiB cache.
  - Neither was tuned beyond that, and neither tuning would add data checksums.
- **The C1-log B8 p99.** On `negative-coords` the p99 was 368 ms. That is one batch in 20, still inside the budget, and not investigated.
