## 2. What changed, and why

**Gates: unchanged.** Every layout passes all three gates:

- B1: 9.5 million records under `-Xmx256m`.
- B2: 0 of 300 random flips silent, on both the read and verify paths.
- B3: 40 of 40 kills reopen consistently, including kills during checkpoints.

**Improvements:**

- **B4 is now met.** Per-unit compression (decision D3) made uniform lookups on 9.5 million records 8× faster with R = 1 (7.9 µs p50) and 4× faster with R = 4 (15.4 µs p50). R = 32 reproduces the baseline's failure (57 µs), which confirms the cause: a cache miss inflated the whole region.
- **Build and maintenance are faster.**

  | Measure (9.5 M records) | Baseline | R = 4 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Bulk build | 176 k/s | 291 k/s |
  | Checkpoint of 25 000 scattered mutations | 18.5 s | 10.0 s |
  | Full verification | 7.0 s | 4.6 s |

  The production checkpoint decodes each touched block once and streams its units, where the spike decoded records twice.
- **Commits are unchanged:** 16 ms p50, one `fdatasync`.

**Regressions, explained rather than re-budgeted:**

| Metric (`millions`) | Baseline | `prod-rows4` | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 39 ms | 197 ms (budget 1 s) | Production opens the way an application must, and the spike did none of this. It takes the sidecar writer lock: writing and syncing `writer.owner.json` and syncing the directory is two `fsync`s, about 32 ms here, plus first-time JSON class loading in a cold JVM. It also re-reads the catalog header to detect a concurrent checkpoint, and explains every file in `chunk-index/` before allowing writes. |
| Disk per record | 20.3 bytes | 21.6 bytes (+6 %); R = 1: 23.4 (+15 %) | Smaller compression units compress a little worse. This is the price of B4, and stays under B11 (sparse 16.0, dense 5.5). |
| Index heap after the workload | 58 MiB | 82 MiB | The cache budget (48 MiB) counts bytes of headers and units. With smaller units there are many more cache entries, and the map's per-entry overhead is not counted. B1 still passes under 256 MiB. A future refinement could include an estimate of that overhead in the budget. |
| Season envelope (max) | 7 ms | 22 ms (budget 2 s) | Not investigated: it is still about 100× under budget. Envelopes come from catalog summaries in both implementations. |
| Full scan (`sparse-exploration`) | 6.6 M/s | 4.9 M/s | Eight unit inflations per region instead of one; still 10× the B6 floor |
| Commit p99, worst dataset | 368 ms | 209 ms (`dense-square`); R = 1: 245 ms | p99 of 20 batches is the slowest batch. Where the update phase crossed the 100 000-mutation threshold, that batch includes a checkpoint (`negative-coords`); elsewhere it is a single slow batch. All are under the 500 ms budget. |

## 3. Choice of the default unit size

**R = 4 rows (128 chunks) per unit.** It meets B4 with margin (15.4 µs p50 and 87 µs p99, against 20 µs and 1 ms), and costs 6 % more disk than whole-region blocks.

R = 1 has more lookup margin but costs 15 % more disk. Disk matters for backups of multi-decade worlds, and lookups at the R = 4 latency are far below anything Paper needs per chunk event.

R is stored in every block header, so the default can change later without a format change or a migration.
