# MCOneWorld — Shared `AGENTS.md` Prefix

> This is the canonical instruction prefix for every MCOneWorld project module. Copy it to the beginning of each module-specific `AGENTS.md`, or keep it at the repository root and make every module-specific `AGENTS.md` explicitly inherit it. Module-specific instructions may add constraints. They may override this prefix only when the override is explicit, justified, and recorded in an architecture decision.

## 1. Project purpose

MCOneWorld provides repeatable Minecraft Java “season” resets while preserving the complete history of earlier seasons in the same underlying world.

A new season begins in distant, previously unused terrain. Previous bases, farms, terrain changes, villagers, portals, inventories archived in-world, and other historical areas remain at their original absolute coordinates and can later be visited intentionally. Routine season creation must not translate old chunks.

The project family has three user-facing deliverables built from one monorepo:

1. **MCOneWorld Tool** — an offline application for inspection, planning, validation, backups, sidecar management, and carefully controlled world-file operations.
2. **MCOneWorld Paper** — one Paper plugin containing all server-side runtime features. Individual capabilities are configuration-controlled modules, not separate plugins.
3. **MCOneWorld Client** — one optional Fabric client mod containing all client-side enhancements. Individual capabilities are configuration-controlled modules, not separate mods.

The Paper plugin may bundle and manage a version-matched stronghold world-generation datapack. That datapack is an implementation resource of the server plugin, not a fourth user-managed project.

## 2. Product principles and non-negotiable invariants

All implementation decisions must preserve these invariants:

- **Vanilla clients remain supported.** A player without MCOneWorld Client must be able to join and play. Essential gameplay, resets, archive access, and security cannot depend on the client mod.
- **Minecraft retains its normal world format.** MCOneWorld metadata lives outside the vanilla world save in a sidecar directory. Do not invent a replacement world format or inject application metadata into chunks, `level.dat`, player NBT, or arbitrary scoreboard entries merely to store MCOneWorld state.
- **Absolute coordinates are authoritative.** Minecraft’s `(0,0)` never moves. A season origin is a logical transform used by MCOneWorld interfaces: `localX = absoluteX - originX` and `localZ = absoluteZ - originZ`.
- **Historical chunks do not move during a normal reset.** Chunk translation is an exceptional migration tool, never part of the standard new-season workflow.
- **One persistent terrain seed is the default architecture.** Do not change the seed during a normal reset.
- **Strongholds can generate globally in new terrain.** The server’s bundled datapack replaces origin-centered stronghold rings with a version-tested random-spread placement. Existing generated chunks are never retroactively regenerated by this feature.
- **Player UUIDs are permanent identities.** Reset season-specific progression associated with a UUID; never replace or fabricate a player UUID to simulate a reset.
- **Old areas are preserved by default.** Every destructive operation requires an explicit target, a preview, a verified backup, and a rollback path.
- **The sidecar is authoritative for MCOneWorld history.** Runtime caches and client state are derived from it and must never silently become competing sources of truth.
- **Features are modular internally, not separate products.** Use feature flags and service boundaries inside the one Paper plugin and one Fabric mod.
- **Removal must fail gracefully.** Removing MCOneWorld must leave a valid vanilla-format world. MCOneWorld-only conveniences may disappear, but preserved terrain and ordinary vanilla gameplay must remain usable.

## 3. Scope boundaries

### In scope

- Named season records and dimension-specific logical origins.
- Distant seasonal spawn planning and validation.
- Conservative chunk inspection, selection, deletion, and regeneration workflows.
- Player progression archival and reset with retained UUIDs.
- Physical inventory and Ender-chest archives, with a safe central-archive fallback.
- Global stronghold generation in newly generated terrain.
- Controlled portals or travel to historical seasons.
- Optional season-local coordinate presentation on Fabric clients.
- Backup manifests, operation journals, validation reports, and rollback support.
- Conversion to an ordinary vanilla-format world by removing MCOneWorld sidecar/plugin/datapack metadata without deleting preserved vanilla chunks.

### Not automatically in scope

- Moving old seasons to make the newest season numerically centered on `(0,0)`.
- Rewriting every coordinate embedded in maps, command blocks, books, signs, lodestones, entity memories, third-party plugins, or unknown mod data.
- Claiming lossless arbitrary chunk translation without version-specific proof and tests.
- Requiring Fabric, NeoForge, a proxy, or any client mod to join the Paper server.
- Silently resetting the central End island or dragon-fight metadata.
- Modifying existing chunks merely because a new world-generation policy was installed.

The End strategy is decided by ADR 0010 (2026-10-02): a new season archives the whole End dimension directory, which in 26.1+ includes the dragon-fight state, into the sidecar's backup area through the Tool's journaled season transition, so a fresh End generates. This is never silent: it is previewed, backed up, verified, and reversible. Outside that operation, preserve all existing End chunks, builds, entities, gateways, and dragon-fight data.

## 4. Monorepo architecture

Use a Gradle multi-project build with Kotlin DSL unless an accepted architecture decision states otherwise.

```text
mconeworld/
├── AGENTS.md
├── settings.gradle.kts
├── build.gradle.kts
├── gradle/
├── docs/
│   ├── architecture/
│   ├── decisions/
│   ├── formats/
│   └── compatibility/
├── shared/
│   ├── schema/
│   ├── protocol/
│   └── test-fixtures/
├── offline-tool/
├── paper-plugin/
│   └── src/main/resources/bundled-datapacks/
└── fabric-client/
```

The exact directory names may evolve, but preserve these dependency rules:

- Shared modules contain pure Java domain types, validation, coordinate math, format codecs, and protocol definitions.
- Shared modules must not depend on Paper, Bukkit, Fabric, Minecraft implementation classes, JavaFX, or MCA Selector internals.
- Platform modules adapt shared types to platform APIs.
- The offline tool and Paper plugin may read and write the sidecar through the same tested shared codec.
- The Fabric client receives only the minimal runtime season context over the network. It does not open the server sidecar.
- Dependency direction always points from platform modules toward shared modules, never the reverse.

Produce three user-facing release artifacts:

```text
MCOneWorld-Tool-<version>.<distribution>
MCOneWorld-Paper-<version>.jar
MCOneWorld-Client-Fabric-<version>.jar
```

Shared libraries and the bundled datapack are implementation details and should not require separate installation.

## 5. Version policy

“Latest stable” is not a reproducible dependency declaration.

- Pin exact Minecraft, Java, Paper API/server, Fabric Loader, Fabric API, Gradle, and library versions in source control.
- Maintain a human-readable compatibility matrix in `docs/compatibility/`.
- Treat each Minecraft data-version change as a compatibility event requiring fixture and migration review.
- Do not use `latest`, dynamic Gradle selectors such as `1.+`, unpinned snapshots, or undocumented internal server mappings in a release build.
- Prefer documented Paper APIs. Isolate unavoidable version-specific/NMS code behind a narrow adapter and fail closed on unknown versions.
- World-generation datapacks must declare the exact supported pack format and be verified against the server version before installation.
- Support claims require automated tests or a recorded manual validation procedure against the exact claimed versions.

## 6. Canonical terminology

Use these terms consistently in code, documentation, UI, and tests:

- **Absolute coordinate:** Minecraft’s real coordinate in a dimension.
- **Season origin:** the dimension-specific absolute coordinate treated as logical `(0,0)` for one season.
- **Season-local coordinate:** a derived presentation coordinate relative to a season origin.
- **Season spawn:** the actual absolute spawn position configured for a season.
- **Season envelope:** a conservative bounding area used for visualization and planning; it is not proof that every enclosed chunk belongs to that season.
- **Chunk provenance:** evidence describing when or during which season a chunk was first observed, generated, or active.
- **Protected chunk:** a chunk that destructive operations must not alter without an explicit override.
- **Archive portal marker:** a sidecar record describing an intended controlled-travel endpoint.
- **Operation plan:** immutable proposed changes and preconditions created before mutation.
- **Operation journal:** durable record of planned, started, completed, failed, and rolled-back steps.
- **Vanilla export:** removal of MCOneWorld-specific external metadata and runtime components while retaining an ordinary playable Minecraft save.

Avoid calling a season origin “the world origin.”

## 7. Shared domain model

Use stable IDs independently of display names. Prefer UUIDs for identity and user-editable strings for names.

The shared model should include, at minimum, equivalents of the following concepts. Names may change only through an intentional schema decision.

```java
record SeasonId(UUID value) {}

record DimensionKey(String namespacedId) {}

record BlockPosition(
    DimensionKey dimension,
    long x,
    int y,
    long z
) {}

record ChunkPosition(
    DimensionKey dimension,
    int x,
    int z
) {}

record DimensionOrigin(
    DimensionKey dimension,
    long x,
    int y,
    long z
) {}

record SeasonRecord(
    SeasonId id,
    String name,
    Instant createdAt,
    Map<DimensionKey, DimensionOrigin> origins,
    BlockPosition spawn
) {}
```

Additional shared concepts should include:

- `ArchiveManifest`
- `FormatVersion`
- `SeasonStatus`
- `ChunkEnvelope`
- `ChunkProvenance`
- `ProtectedRegion`
- `PortalMarker`
- `BackupRecord`
- `PlayerArchiveRecord`
- `OperationId`
- `OperationPlan`
- `OperationJournal`
- `ValidationIssue`
- `FeatureFlags`
- `ProtocolVersion`
- `SeasonContext`

### Shared type rules

- Use `long` for coordinate calculations and serialized absolute X/Z values, then validate before conversion to Minecraft API types.
- Use `int` for chunk coordinates only after checked floor-division by 16.
- Never use truncating division for negative block-to-chunk conversion. Use floor division.
- Represent dimensions as validated namespaced identifiers such as `minecraft:overworld`, not enums limited to the three vanilla dimensions.
- Store timestamps as UTC instants serialized in ISO 8601.
- Distinguish absent, unknown, and intentionally unset values. Do not overload zero or an empty string as “unknown.”
- Display names are not keys and need not be unique.
- Serialization order should be deterministic to support reviewable diffs and repeatable checksums.

## 8. Coordinate rules

Absolute coordinates remain authoritative everywhere.

```text
seasonLocalX = absoluteX - seasonOriginX
seasonLocalZ = absoluteZ - seasonOriginZ
```

Recommended season origins are aligned to 128 Overworld blocks:

```text
originX mod 128 = 0
originZ mod 128 = 0
```

This keeps the Overworld origin chunk-aligned and its vanilla-scaled Nether counterpart aligned to a Nether chunk:

```text
netherOriginX = overworldOriginX / 8
netherOriginZ = overworldOriginZ / 8
```

Do not silently round a misaligned origin. Report it and require an explicit decision.

Every coordinate-bearing record must identify its dimension. The End does not inherit the Overworld transform. If a dimension has no configured season origin, clients and commands must display absolute coordinates.

Coordinate presentation never changes world mechanics. Commands, maps, lodestones, portals, entities, and world generation continue to use absolute coordinates.

## 9. Sidecar storage contract

MCOneWorld uses a **hybrid sidecar directory** outside the Minecraft save directories.

```text
mconeworld-data/
├── manifest.json
├── seasons/
├── indexes/
├── player-archives/
├── operations/
├── backups/
└── reports/
```

The division of responsibility is:

- Human-reviewable, low-volume metadata uses deterministic, versioned JSON.
- Potentially large per-chunk indexes use a compact, explicitly versioned indexed format selected and documented by the shared-schema module.
- Binary/indexed records must have checksums and a documented export path to a readable diagnostic representation.
- The manifest references data files by relative path and checksum; it must not contain machine-specific absolute filesystem paths.
- Unknown fields from a newer compatible schema must not be silently discarded during a read-modify-write cycle.
- Writes use temporary files, flush/sync where appropriate, checksum verification, and atomic replacement when supported.
- A partially written sidecar must be detectable and recoverable from its journal or previous manifest.
- Schema migrations are explicit, versioned, testable, and never run implicitly during a destructive world operation.

Do not finalize the compact chunk-index encoding incidentally inside a feature change. It requires its own documented decision, benchmarks, corruption tests, and migration strategy.

### Chunk provenance is not exclusive ownership

A chunk may be generated in one season and revisited or modified during another. Do not model season membership as a single unquestionable owner.

At minimum, distinguish:

- First observed/generated season, if known.
- Last observed activity season, if known.
- Evidence source and confidence.
- Protected status.
- Whether the chunk existed before MCOneWorld tracking began.

For an imported vanilla world, mark pre-existing chunks as legacy/unknown provenance rather than pretending their exact season is known.

## 10. Safe world-operation protocol

Any operation that can alter world files or meaningful player state follows a two-phase workflow:

1. **Inspect:** identify server version, data version, dimensions, paths, locks, and existing sidecar state.
2. **Plan:** create an immutable operation plan with exact targets, reasons, preconditions, estimated effects, and backup requirements.
3. **Preview:** show maps/lists/counts and validation warnings without mutation.
4. **Back up:** create or verify a restorable backup and record its identifier and checksum.
5. **Apply:** execute only the approved plan against an offline or appropriately quiesced world.
6. **Validate:** reopen outputs, verify structural invariants, and run version-appropriate checks.
7. **Commit:** atomically update the sidecar and mark the journal complete.
8. **Rollback:** on failure, stop, preserve diagnostics, and restore or provide exact restoration instructions.

Rules:

- Dry-run is the default for destructive commands.
- Never edit a world while it is open by a Minecraft server unless the relevant operation is explicitly designed and tested as an online Paper operation.
- Detect lock files and refuse unsafe offline writes.
- Never overwrite the only copy of a world.
- Never treat “command exited successfully” as sufficient validation.
- Never delete chunks based only on rectangular containment, file age, or a single activity statistic.
- Preserve region, entity, and POI data as a coordinated set.
- Unknown NBT or data components must be preserved when round-tripping unless a version-specific migration explicitly handles them.
- Every destructive operation must be idempotent or detect that it was already applied.

## 11. Player identity, archival, and reset rules

- Preserve the player’s UUID and account identity.
- Archive before clearing anything.
- Prefer the online Paper plugin for version-aware item serialization and placement while the server owns runtime player state.
- Preserve item components/NBT, enchantments, names, lore, durability, nested shulker contents, container contents, custom model/data components, and plugin-provided item metadata as faithfully as the supported server API permits.
- Maintain a redundant sidecar archive record before placing physical containers. Physical chests are the player-facing archive; the sidecar snapshot and operation journal support verification and recovery.
- Do not destroy or replace a player’s bed by default. Place labeled containers safely nearby, or use the configured central museum/archive fallback.
- Container placement must check block occupancy, support, claims/protection integrations when enabled, chunk availability, and rollback behavior.
- Reset personal respawn state without breaking the historical bed or respawn-anchor block.
- Clear or reset configured progression categories only after their archives and plan are verified.
- Homes, warps, back locations, teleport requests, grave locations, and other third-party shortcuts require explicit integrations or an administrator-supplied policy. Never claim they were reset if MCOneWorld cannot verify them.

## 12. Stronghold world-generation policy

The default design uses one persistent seed plus globally distributed strongholds in newly generated terrain.

- Implement placement through a version-matched datapack using Minecraft’s structure-set registry, not ad-hoc post-generation block placement.
- Bundle the datapack in MCOneWorld Paper and manage installation, compatibility checks, checksum verification, and conflict detection from that single plugin.
- Do not silently overwrite another datapack that modifies the stronghold structure set.
- Existing chunks and existing strongholds remain unchanged.
- Document that the distribution is no longer vanilla’s finite origin-centered ring distribution, although generated strongholds remain normal strongholds.
- Keep the datapack installed consistently after adoption. Removing or changing placement requires a planned compatibility operation.
- Test Eye of Ender behavior, `/locate`, portal-room generation, and generation in previously ungenerated chunks on every supported Minecraft version.

## 13. Paper plugin contract

MCOneWorld Paper is the runtime authority for the active season.

It may provide internally toggled modules for:

- Sidecar loading, validation, locking, and safe runtime updates.
- Applying a season activation: world spawn, then player archival and reset. The Tool activates a season offline and marks it pending application (ADR 0011); the plugin applies it at startup and clears the marker.
- Player inventory/Ender-chest archival and progression reset.
- Respawn-policy enforcement.
- Archive portal and hub behavior.
- Stronghold datapack installation and verification.
- Vanilla fallback commands such as `/season coords` and season-history queries.
- Optional client-mod synchronization.
- Integrations with explicitly supported home, warp, claim, map, or permissions plugins.

Feature modules must communicate through internal service interfaces and must not become separately installed plugin JARs.

If the sidecar is missing, corrupt, unsupported, or inconsistent with the loaded worlds, disable mutating MCOneWorld features and provide actionable diagnostics. Do not guess or create a new history over an existing world automatically.

## 14. Optional Fabric client contract

MCOneWorld Client is an optional presentation and convenience layer.

- It must never be required for joining or completing essential gameplay.
- It does not directly read the server filesystem or sidecar.
- It initiates an optional protocol handshake. The Paper plugin replies only to clients that advertise support, so vanilla clients are unaffected.
- The server remains authoritative for season ID, name, revision, and dimension origins.
- Coordinate display has at least `GLOBAL`, `SEASON_LOCAL`, and preferably `BOTH` modes.
- Local coordinates must be clearly labeled to prevent them from being mistaken for command coordinates.
- If the server provides no valid origin for the current dimension, display global coordinates.
- Cache settings per server identity. Treat cached season context as stale until confirmed after reconnecting.
- Prefer additive debug/HUD rendering for compatibility. Any exact replacement of vanilla F3 lines must be isolated behind a feature flag and tested with common F3 customization mods.

## 15. Network protocol rules

The client/server protocol is versioned independently from the sidecar schema.

Minimum logical messages:

- `mconeworld:hello`: client capabilities and protocol versions.
- `mconeworld:context`: active season ID, display name, sidecar revision, and dimension origins.
- A future capabilities message may advertise optional portal/history UI data without making it mandatory.

Requirements:

- Use a namespaced channel owned by MCOneWorld.
- Bound all strings, collections, and payload sizes.
- Validate identifiers and numeric ranges before storing or displaying them.
- Ignore unsupported optional fields safely.
- Reject incompatible protocol versions gracefully without disconnecting an otherwise valid vanilla-compatible player unless a security issue requires it.
- Never trust client-supplied season, coordinate, payment, unlock, or teleport state.
- Do not send custom payloads to a client until it has advertised compatible support.
- Maintain golden byte-level protocol fixtures for every supported protocol version.

## 16. Portals and historical travel

- Portal destinations use absolute, dimension-qualified coordinates stored in the sidecar.
- Historical travel is deliberately unlocked or invoked; it must not become an accidental early-season shortcut.
- Validate destination safety before teleporting.
- Do not rely on vanilla Nether portal linking for arbitrary archive-hub routing.
- Keep custom archive portals logically distinct from ordinary Nether portals.
- Costs, unlocks, permissions, and return behavior are server-authoritative and independently configurable.
- Provide administrative recovery commands that do not depend on a functioning portal structure.
- Never delete or rewrite historical vanilla portals as part of archive setup.

## 17. Testing expectations

No module is complete merely because it compiles.

### Shared-module tests

- Coordinate transforms, including negative values and boundary values.
- Block/chunk/region conversions using floor division.
- Overworld/Nether alignment and overflow checks.
- Deterministic serialization and checksums.
- Schema parsing, validation, migrations, unknown-field behavior, and corrupt-input rejection.
- Operation-plan and journal state machines.
- Protocol round trips and golden binary fixtures.
- Property-based tests where coordinate or migration state spaces are large.

### World-data tests

- Maintain small, legally redistributable fixtures for every supported Minecraft data version.
- Include chunks with entities, POIs, villagers, containers, signs, item frames, maps, lodestones, scheduled ticks, structures, and negative coordinates.
- Never mutate canonical fixtures in place; copy them into a temporary directory per test.
- Compare semantic NBT as well as raw file integrity where appropriate.
- Test interruption between every journaled mutation stage and prove recovery behavior.

### Paper integration tests

- Start an exact pinned Paper build in an isolated temporary server directory.
- Test first installation, restart, upgrade, missing/corrupt sidecar, datapack conflict, and rollback.
- Test both vanilla clients and compatible modded-client handshakes.
- Verify player archive fidelity with complex nested items.
- Verify reset behavior for online and offline players where supported.
- Verify world spawn, personal respawn, Nether portal isolation, permissions, and archive-portal safeguards.
- Verify global stronghold generation, Eye of Ender targeting, `/locate`, and End portal activation in new chunks.

### Fabric client tests

- Test global/local/both modes in every configured dimension.
- Test negative coordinates, origin changes, reconnects, stale caches, absent plugin, and incompatible protocol versions.
- Test that joining an unrelated or vanilla server behaves normally.
- Test keybinding conflicts and the configured compatibility behavior with supported F3/HUD mods.

### Release validation

- Build from a clean checkout with the committed wrapper and lockfiles.
- Run unit, property, fixture, integration, and migration tests appropriate to the changed modules.
- Generate checksums and a compatibility report.
- Exercise backup and rollback on a disposable copy of a representative long-lived world.
- Never claim support for a Minecraft/Paper/Fabric version that has not passed the recorded validation suite.

## 18. Development workflow for AI agents

When working in any MCOneWorld module:

1. Read this shared prefix, the nearest module-specific `AGENTS.md`, relevant architecture decisions, and the current compatibility matrix completely.
2. Inspect the existing code and tests before proposing changes. Do not assume the repository matches a prompt written earlier.
3. State the exact scope, invariants affected, and acceptance criteria.
4. Identify whether the task is read-only, reversible, world-mutating, sidecar-mutating, or destructive.
5. For non-trivial work, write or update a small implementation plan and keep only one step in progress.
6. Implement the smallest cohesive change that satisfies the current milestone. Do not opportunistically rewrite unrelated systems.
7. Add tests before or alongside behavior. A regression fix requires a test that fails without the fix when practical.
8. Run the narrow tests first, then the relevant module and integration suites.
9. Update schemas, examples, compatibility documentation, and architecture decisions when behavior or contracts change.
10. Report what changed, what was verified, remaining risks, and any manual test steps.

Agents must not:

- Guess undocumented NBT layouts or internal Minecraft behavior when source, generated reports, fixtures, or an executable test can verify it.
- Introduce hidden global state, implicit migrations, or automatic destructive “repair.”
- Disable failing tests to complete a milestone.
- broaden supported-version claims without evidence.
- Commit generated worlds, credentials, server EULAs, proprietary server JARs, or large transient test outputs.
- Treat Paper’s vanilla-parity configuration as permission to change gameplay semantics unrelated to MCOneWorld.

If requirements conflict, preserve data first, stop mutation, document the conflict, and request a decision.

## 19. Coding and API quality

- Prefer explicit immutable domain values over primitive parameter lists.
- Keep filesystem, NBT, platform API, UI, and domain logic behind separate interfaces.
- Use dependency injection at module boundaries; avoid service locators and globally accessible plugin instances in domain code.
- Return structured validation errors with severity, code, location, and remediation—not only log strings.
- Make cancellation and interruption safe for long scans and generation tasks.
- Keep heavy filesystem work off the Paper main thread. Interact with Bukkit/Paper world state only on the appropriate server thread or scheduler context.
- Bound memory usage; long-lived worlds may contain millions of chunks.
- Stream scans and writes where possible.
- Use structured logging with operation and season IDs, while avoiding unnecessary player-sensitive data.
- Public formats and protocols require examples and compatibility tests.
- Comments should explain invariants and non-obvious reasons, not narrate straightforward code.

## 20. Security and trust boundaries

- Treat worlds, sidecars, imported archives, datapacks, and client payloads as untrusted input.
- Prevent path traversal when resolving manifest-relative paths or extracting bundled resources.
- Bound decompression, collection sizes, recursion, NBT depth, and network payloads.
- Do not deserialize arbitrary Java objects.
- Validate permissions for every administrative or teleport command.
- Do not allow a client to select an unauthorized season destination or bypass configured travel costs.
- Resolve symlinks and filesystem roots deliberately before destructive operations.
- Refuse ambiguous paths and overlapping source/backup/output locations.

## 21. Documentation and decision discipline

Record significant choices as short architecture decision records under `docs/decisions/`, including:

- Compact chunk-index encoding.
- Exact sidecar JSON schemas and migration guarantees.
- Initial supported Minecraft/Paper/Fabric/Java versions.
- Offline-tool relationship to MCA Selector: fork, reused library code, subprocess integration, or independent implementation.
- End preservation and seasonal progression strategy.
- Player item serialization and recovery format.
- Archive portal implementation and hub location/dimension.
- Stronghold spacing/separation defaults.
- F3 injection strategy and compatibility policy.

Each decision records context, selected option, alternatives, consequences, migration impact, and how the decision is tested.

## 22. Deferred choices

Do not silently decide the following while implementing an unrelated feature:

- ~~Whether the offline tool is an MCA Selector fork or an independent application.~~ Resolved by ADR 0009: a new application that vendors selected MCA Selector code.
- The final compact chunk-index encoding.
- Whether the archive hub is in protected distant terrain or a separate custom dimension/world.
- ~~Whether each season receives a separate End world/dimension.~~ Resolved by ADR 0010: the End is archived and regenerated per season. A separate End dimension per season may still be added later.
- Which third-party home, warp, claim, map, or permission plugins receive first-class integrations.
- The default stronghold density and exact portal-unlock recipe/cost.
- Whether exact F3-line replacement is supported in addition to an appended local-coordinate line.

Until a deferred choice is resolved, create an interface or explicit unsupported state rather than embedding a provisional assumption into persistent data.

## 23. Definition of done

A milestone is complete only when:

- Its acceptance criteria and relevant invariants are satisfied.
- New behavior has automated tests at the appropriate layers.
- Failure, cancellation, and rollback paths have been considered and tested where mutation is involved.
- Supported versions are pinned and validated.
- Persistent-format or protocol changes are versioned and documented.
- Vanilla-client compatibility is retained.
- The world and sidecar remain recoverable after simulated interruption.
- User-facing documentation describes prerequisites, preview, backup, execution, validation, and rollback.
- Known limitations are stated precisely without implying unsupported preservation guarantees.
